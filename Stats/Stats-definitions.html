CS-1 — Descriptive Statistics · source: S1-26_ISM_CS-1_PPT.pdf

Statistics

The science of collecting, organising, analysing, and inferring from data to support decision-making.

ISM CS-1 · p8

Quantitative (numerical) data

Data measured in numbers, either discrete (countable) or continuous (measured on a scale).

ISM CS-1 · p9,11

Qualitative (categorical) data

Data expressing a category or attribute (e.g. eye colour, religion) rather than a numerical amount.

ISM CS-1 · p9,11

Nominal / Ordinal / Interval / Ratio scales

The four measurement levels: nominal (unordered categories), ordinal (ordered categories), interval (meaningful differences, no true zero), ratio (true zero, ratios meaningful).

ISM CS-1 · p10

Measure of central tendency

A single value describing the centre of a data set — the mean, median or mode.

ISM CS-1 · p13

Mean

The arithmetic average; the sum of values divided by their count. Uses every value and is sensitive to outliers.

ISM CS-1 · p14

Median

The middle value of ordered data (the 50th percentile); half the values lie below it. Robust to outliers.

ISM CS-1 · p20

Mode

The value or category with the highest frequency; a data set may have none, one, or several modes.

ISM CS-1 · p18

Measure of variability

A quantity describing the spread or dispersion of scores — range, variance, standard deviation, or quartile deviation.

ISM CS-1 · p27–28

Range

The distance between the largest and smallest values; based on only two data points.

ISM CS-1 · p29

Variance

The mean of the squared deviations of scores from their mean; variability measured in squared units.

ISM CS-1 · p34

Standard deviation

The square root of the variance; the average distance of scores from the mean, in the original units.

ISM CS-1 · p30,35

Sum of squares (SS)

The total of squared deviations from the mean, \(\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2\); the numerator of variance.

ISM CS-1 · p33

Skewness

Asymmetry of a distribution: right (positive) skew has a long right tail (mean > median), left (negative) skew a long left tail (mean < median).

ISM CS-1 · p23

Bimodal / Multimodal

A distribution with two distinct modes is bimodal; with more than two, multimodal.

ISM CS-1 · p23; Webinar 1 · s6

Quartiles (Q1, Q2, Q3)

Values dividing ordered data into four equal parts: Q1 = 25% mark, Q2 = median, Q3 = 75% mark.

ISM CS-1 · p43–44

Five-number summary

Minimum, Q1, median, Q3, and maximum — a compact summary of a data set's location and spread.

ISM CS-1 · p43

Interquartile range (IQR)

The spread of the middle 50% of data, Q3 − Q1; unaffected by extreme values.

ISM CS-1 · p44

Quartile deviation

Half the interquartile range, IQR/2; the semi-interquartile spread.

ISM CS-1 · p44

Outlier

A value beyond Q1 − 1.5·IQR or Q3 + 1.5·IQR; a major/extreme outlier lies beyond the 3·IQR fences.

ISM CS-1 · p46–48

Box-and-whisker plot

A graphical display of the five-number summary with a box (Q1–Q3), a median line, whiskers, and marked outliers.

ISM CS-1 · p45

Coefficient of variation (CV)

The ratio of standard deviation to mean (SD/mean), a scale-free measure of relative variability.

CS-2 — Probability Basics · source: S1-26_ISM_CS-2_PPT.pdf

Random experiment

An action whose outcome is uncertain before it is performed.

ISM CS-2 · p40

Sample space

The set \(S\) of all possible outcomes of a random experiment.

ISM CS-2 · p6,40

Event

Any subset of the sample space; a collection of outcomes that either occurs or does not on a given trial.

ISM CS-2 · p7,40

Equally likely outcomes

Outcomes that all have the same probability.

ISM CS-2 · p40

Complement of an event

The event \(A^c\) consisting of all sample points not in \(A\).

ISM CS-2 · p8

Union of events

\(A\cup B\): the event containing all points in \(A\), in \(B\), or in both.

ISM CS-2 · p9

Intersection of events

\(A\cap B\): the event containing all points that are in both \(A\) and \(B\).

ISM CS-2 · p9

Mutually exclusive events

Events with no common outcomes; they cannot occur at the same time, so \(P(A\cap B)=0\).

ISM CS-2 · p10,40

Independent events

Events where the occurrence of one has no effect on the probability of the other.

ISM CS-2 · p17,40

Classical probability

Probability defined as favourable outcomes divided by total equally likely outcomes.

ISM CS-2 · p11

Empirical probability

Probability estimated as the long-run relative frequency of an event from observed data.

ISM CS-2 · p12

Axiomatic probability

Probability defined by the axioms: non-negativity, \(P(S)=1\), and additivity over disjoint events.

ISM CS-2 · p13

Permutation

An ordered arrangement of \(r\) items chosen from \(n\), counted by \(^nP_r=n!/(n-r)!\).

ISM CS-2 · p33 (counting)

Combination

An unordered selection of \(r\) items from \(n\), counted by \(\binom{n}{r}=n!/[r!(n-r)!]\).

ISM CS-2 · p33,36
CS-3 — Conditional & Total Probability · source: S1-26_ISM_CS-3_PPT.pdf

Conditional probability

The probability of event \(B\) given that \(A\) has occurred, \(P(B\mid A)=P(A\cap B)/P(A)\).

ISM CS-3 · p6

Conditioning event

The event assumed to have occurred (the "given") in a conditional probability.

ISM CS-3 · p5

Multiplication rule

The rule expressing a joint probability as \(P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B\mid A)\), extendable to several events.

ISM CS-3 · p8

Sampling with / without replacement

With replacement, each draw is returned so trials stay independent; without replacement, the pool shrinks and draws become dependent.

ISM CS-3 · p19,23

Partition of a sample space

A set of mutually exclusive events whose union is the whole sample space.

ISM CS-3 · p26

Total probability theorem

The rule \(P(B)=\sum_i P(A_i)P(B\mid A_i)\) over a partition \(\{A_i\}\); also called the rule of elimination.

ISM CS-3 · p26; ISM CS-4 · p7

Tree diagram

A branching diagram whose paths represent sequential events and whose branch products give joint probabilities.

ISM CS-3 · p28
CS-4 — Bayes' Theorem & Naïve Bayes · source: S1-26_ISM_CS-4_PPT.pdf

Bayes' theorem

A rule that inverts conditional probabilities to find \(P(\text{cause}\mid\text{effect})\) from \(P(\text{effect}\mid\text{cause})\), priors and the total probability.

ISM CS-4 · p5,15

Prior probability

\(P(H)\): the probability of a hypothesis before any evidence is observed.

ISM CS-4 · p15

Likelihood

\(P(E\mid H)\): the probability of observing evidence \(E\) assuming hypothesis \(H\) is true.

ISM CS-4 · p15

Posterior probability

\(P(H\mid E)\): the updated probability of a hypothesis after observing the evidence.

ISM CS-4 · p15

Evidence (normalising constant)

\(P(E)\): the total probability of the evidence, ensuring posteriors sum to 1.

ISM CS-4 · p15,25

MAP hypothesis

The maximum a posteriori hypothesis — the one with the largest posterior probability, chosen as the prediction.

ISM CS-4 · p20,29,57

ML estimate

The maximum-likelihood hypothesis — the one maximising \(P(E\mid H)\), ignoring priors.

ISM CS-4 · p57

Naïve Bayes classifier

A Bayesian classifier assuming all features are conditionally independent given the class, so their likelihoods multiply.

ISM CS-4 · p24,29

Conditional independence

\(X\) is conditionally independent of \(Y\) given \(Z\) if \(P(X\mid Y,Z)=P(X\mid Z)\).

ISM CS-4 · p27

Generative model

A model that learns \(P(X\mid Y)\) and \(P(Y)\) to obtain the posterior \(P(Y\mid X)\) via Bayes' rule.

ISM CS-4 · p26

Laplace smoothing

Adding a constant (usually 1) to every frequency count so no conditional probability is zero.

ISM CS-4 · p34,57
CS-5 — Random Variables & Distributions · source: S1-26_ISM_CS-5_PPT.pdf

Random variable

A function assigning a real number to each outcome in the sample space.

ISM CS-5 · p3

Discrete random variable

A random variable taking a countable set of values (e.g. number of heads in two tosses).

ISM CS-5 · p4

Continuous random variable

A random variable taking any value in an interval of the real line (e.g. height, waiting time).

ISM CS-5 · p4,23

Probability mass function (pmf)

For a discrete RV, \(f(x)=P(X=x)\), with \(f(x)\ge 0\) and \(\sum f(x)=1\).

ISM CS-5 · p6–7

Probability density function (pdf)

For a continuous RV, a function whose area over an interval gives the probability of that interval.

ISM CS-5 · p23

Discrete uniform distribution

A distribution assigning equal probability \(1/n\) to each of \(n\) values.

ISM CS-5 · p8

Mathematical expectation

The probability-weighted average \(E[X]=\sum x\,f(x)\); the long-run mean of a random variable.

ISM CS-5 · p10

Fair game

A game whose expected gain is zero.

ISM CS-5 · p11

Cumulative distribution function (CDF)

\(F(x)=P(X\le x)\), the accumulated probability up to \(x\).

ISM CS-5 · p15

Joint probability distribution

The distribution describing two (or more) random variables together, \(f(x,y)=P(X=x,Y=y)\); a bivariate distribution.

ISM CS-5 · p34–35

Marginal probability

The distribution of one variable obtained by summing/integrating the joint over the other, reported in the table margins.

ISM CS-5 · p38

Conditional distribution

The distribution of one RV given a fixed value of another, \(f(y\mid x)=f(x,y)/f_X(x)\).

ISM CS-5 · p41

Independent random variables

Random variables whose joint distribution factorises into the product of their marginals.

ISM CS-5 · p40
EC Exams — Distributions, Inference & Forecasting · source: EC-2 & EC-3 instruction sheets

Binomial distribution

The distribution of the number of successes in \(n\) independent trials each with success probability \(p\).

EC-2 · Q6

Poisson distribution

The distribution of the number of rare events in a fixed interval with mean \(\lambda\); approximates the binomial for large \(n\), small \(p\).

EC-2 · Q6

Normal distribution

A symmetric bell-shaped continuous distribution defined by its mean \(\mu\) and standard deviation \(\sigma\).

EC-3 · Q5,Q8

z-score

The number of standard deviations a value lies from the mean, \(z=(x-\mu)/\sigma\).

EC-3 · Q5

Central limit theorem

The result that the sampling distribution of the sample mean approaches normality as \(n\) grows, regardless of the population shape.

Module 4 scope

Standard error

The standard deviation of a sample statistic; for the mean, \(SE=\sigma/\sqrt{n}\).

Module 4 scope

Null & alternative hypotheses

\(H_0\) is the statement of "no effect / no difference" tested; \(H_1\) is the claim accepted if \(H_0\) is rejected.

EC-3 · Q5

Significance level

\(\alpha\): the probability of rejecting a true null hypothesis (Type I error), e.g. 5%.

EC-3 · Q5–Q6

p-value

The probability, if \(H_0\) is true, of a test statistic at least as extreme as observed; small values favour \(H_1\).

Module 4 scope

Confidence interval

A range of values that contains the population parameter with a stated confidence level.

Module 4 scope

ANOVA

Analysis of variance: an F-test comparing three or more population means by partitioning total variance into between- and within-group components.

EC-3 · Q6

Chi-square test

A test comparing observed and expected frequencies to assess goodness of fit or independence.

Module 4 scope

Correlation coefficient

A number in [−1, 1] measuring the strength and direction of the linear association between two variables.

EC-3 · Q4

Spearman's rank correlation

A correlation computed on the ranks of data, measuring monotonic association.

EC-3 · Q4

Regression line

The least-squares line \(\hat y=b_0+b_1x\) predicting \(y\) from \(x\).

Module 5 scope

Coefficient of determination (R²)

The proportion of variance in the response explained by the regression model.

Module 5 scope

Exponential smoothing

A forecasting method blending the latest observation with the previous forecast via a smoothing constant \(\alpha\).

EC-3 · Q7

Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM)

A probabilistic model representing a density as a weighted sum of Gaussian components with mixing coefficients summing to 1.

EC-3 · Q8
Recovered from image-only slides · source: ISM CS image slides

Law of large numbers

The principle that over a large number of trials the empirical (relative-frequency) probability of an event approaches its true probability; more observations give a more accurate estimate.

ISM CS-2 · p12 (image)

Probability function

The function \(f(x)\) (or \(p(x)\)) giving the probability for each value of a random variable; a distribution can be described by a table, graph, or equation.
