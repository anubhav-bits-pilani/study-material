CS-1 — Descriptive Statistics · source: S1-26_ISM_CS-1_PPT.pdf

Sample mean (ungrouped)

$$\bar{Y}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} Y_i$$

where: \(Y_i\) = each observation; \(n\) = number of observations.

use: the arithmetic average of quantitative data with no extreme outliers.

ISM CS-1 · p14–15

Mean for grouped / frequency data

$$\bar{Y}=\frac{\sum f\,Y}{N}$$

where: \(Y\) = a class value/score; \(f\) = its frequency; \(N=\sum f\) = total frequency.

use: mean when data are given as a frequency distribution.

ISM CS-1 · p14,16

Median (positional)

$$\text{Median}=\begin{cases}Y_{\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)}, & n\text{ odd}\\[4pt] \dfrac{Y_{(n/2)}+Y_{(n/2+1)}}{2}, & n\text{ even}\end{cases}$$

where: values sorted ascending; subscript = rank position.

use: robust centre for skewed data or ordinal scales; unaffected by outliers.

ISM CS-1 · p20–22

Empirical mode relation

$$\text{Mode}=3\,\text{Median}-2\,\text{Mean}$$

where: applies to moderately skewed distributions.

use: quick estimate of the mode from mean and median for unimodal, moderately skewed data.

ISM CS-1 · p23

Range

$$R = X_{\max}-X_{\min}$$

where: \(X_{\max},X_{\min}\) = largest and smallest values (real limits URL/LRL for continuous data).

use: quickest, crudest spread measure; sensitive to extremes.

ISM CS-1 · p29

Sum of squares (SS)

$$SS=\sum_{i=1}^{N}(X_i-\bar{X})^2$$

where: \(X_i\) = observation; \(\bar X\) = mean.

use: building block for both variance and standard deviation.

ISM CS-1 · p33,39

Population variance & SD

$$\sigma^2=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}(X_i-\mu)^2}{N},\qquad \sigma=\sqrt{\sigma^2}$$

where: \(\mu\) = population mean; \(N\) = population size.

use: spread when the whole population is measured.

ISM CS-1 · p34–36

Sample variance & SD (n−1)

$$s^2=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_i-\bar{X})^2}{n-1}=\frac{SS}{n-1},\qquad s=\sqrt{s^2}$$

where: \(\bar X\) = sample mean; \(n-1\) = degrees of freedom (Bessel's correction).

use: unbiased estimate of population variability from a sample (e.g. SS = 24, n = 4 → \(s^2=8\)).

ISM CS-1 · p38–41

Quartile positions

$$\text{Pos}(Q_1)=\frac{1(n+1)}{4},\qquad \text{Pos}(Q_3)=\frac{3(n+1)}{4}$$

where: \(n\) = number of ordered observations; interpolate between ranks if non-integer.

use: locate the 25% and 75% marks for the five-number summary.

ISM CS-1 · p44

Interquartile range & quartile deviation

$$IQR = Q_3-Q_1,\qquad QD=\frac{IQR}{2}$$

where: \(Q_1,Q_3\) = first and third quartiles.

use: robust spread of the middle 50%, unaffected by extreme values.

ISM CS-1 · p44

Outlier fences (1.5·IQR rule)

$$\text{Lower}=Q_1-1.5\,IQR,\qquad \text{Upper}=Q_3+1.5\,IQR$$

where: values outside the fences are potential outliers; using 3·IQR gives major/extreme outliers.

use: flag outliers and set box-plot whisker limits.

ISM CS-1 · p45–48

Coefficient of variation

$$CV=\frac{s}{\bar{x}}\times 100\%$$

where: \(s\) = standard deviation; \(\bar x\) = mean.

use: compare relative variability across variables on different scales/units.

CS-2 — Probability Rules & Counting · source: S1-26_ISM_CS-2_PPT.pdf

Classical probability

$$P(A)=\frac{\text{number of favourable outcomes}}{\text{total number of equally likely outcomes}}$$

where: outcomes assumed equally likely.

use: dice, cards, coins — finite equally-likely sample spaces.

ISM CS-2 · p11

Axioms of probability

$$0\le P(A)\le 1,\quad P(S)=1,\quad P\!\left(\bigcup_i A_i\right)=\sum_i P(A_i)\ \text{for disjoint }A_i$$

where: \(S\) = sample space; \(A_i\) = mutually exclusive events.

use: the rules any valid probability assignment must satisfy.

ISM CS-2 · p13,19

Complement rule

$$P(A^c)=1-P(A)$$

where: \(A^c\) = "not A".

use: "at least one" problems are easiest via the complement.

ISM CS-2 · p8; ISM CS-3 · p20

Addition rule

$$P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)$$

where: subtract the overlap once; if mutually exclusive, \(P(A\cap B)=0\).

use: probability that A or B (or both) occurs; rearrange to get \(P(A\cap B)\).

ISM CS-2 · p15,28

Exactly one of two events

$$P(\text{exactly one})=P(A)+P(B)-2P(A\cap B)$$

where: equals \(P(A\cup B)-P(A\cap B)\).

use: "exactly one service / one constituency" style questions.

ISM CS-2 · p35; Webinar 1 · s20

Permutations & combinations

$$^nP_r=\frac{n!}{(n-r)!},\qquad \binom{n}{r}={}^nC_r=\frac{n!}{r!\,(n-r)!}$$

where: \(n\) = total items; \(r\) = chosen; permutations count ordered, combinations unordered selections.

use: count favourable/total outcomes (e.g. committee of 5 from 8 men and 4 women).

ISM CS-2 · p33,36
CS-3 — Conditional & Total Probability · source: S1-26_ISM_CS-3_PPT.pdf

Conditional probability

$$P(B\mid A)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(A)},\quad P(A)>0$$

where: \(A\) = conditioning (given) event; \(B\) = event of interest.

use: revise a probability after learning event A occurred.

ISM CS-3 · p6

Multiplication rule

$$P(A\cap B)=P(A)\,P(B\mid A)=P(B)\,P(A\mid B)$$ $$P(A\cap B\cap C)=P(A)\,P(B\mid A)\,P(C\mid A\cap B)$$

where: chains dependent events in sequence.

use: sampling without replacement; joint probability of a sequence of events.

ISM CS-3 · p8

Independence condition

$$P(A\cap B)=P(A)\,P(B)\iff P(B\mid A)=P(B)$$

where: holds only when A gives no information about B.

use: test independence; compute joint probability of independent events by multiplying.

ISM CS-3 · p16

Total probability theorem

$$P(B)=\sum_{i=1}^{k} P(A_i)\,P(B\mid A_i)$$

where: \(A_1,\dots,A_k\) partition \(S\) (mutually exclusive, \(\bigcup A_i=S\)).

use: overall probability of B by summing over all "causes" \(A_i\) (tree diagram).

ISM CS-3 · p26–28,40
CS-4 — Bayes' Theorem & Naïve Bayes · source: S1-26_ISM_CS-4_PPT.pdf

Bayes' theorem (partition form)

$$P(E_i\mid A)=\frac{P(E_i)\,P(A\mid E_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^{n} P(E_j)\,P(A\mid E_j)}$$

where: \(E_1,\dots,E_n\) = mutually exclusive, exhaustive causes; \(A\) = observed event.

use: find the probability of a particular cause given an observed effect (e.g. which machine produced a defective item).

ISM CS-4 · p5,8

Bayes for a hypothesis

$$P(H\mid E)=\frac{P(E\mid H)\,P(H)}{P(E)}$$

where: \(P(H)\) = prior; \(P(E\mid H)\) = likelihood; \(P(E)\) = evidence (normaliser); \(P(H\mid E)\) = posterior.

use: update belief in hypothesis H after observing evidence E.

ISM CS-4 · p15

Naïve Bayes classifier

$$P(Y=y_k\mid X_1,\dots,X_n)=\frac{P(y_k)\prod_{i=1}^{n} P(X_i\mid y_k)}{\sum_{j} P(y_j)\prod_{i=1}^{n} P(X_i\mid y_j)}$$

where: \(y_k\) = class; \(X_i\) = features assumed conditionally independent given the class.

use: classify by comparing numerators across classes (spam vs normal, play vs no-play).

ISM CS-4 · p28–29,32

MAP decision rule

$$\hat{Y}=\arg\max_{y_k}\; P(y_k)\prod_{i} P(X_i\mid y_k)$$

where: choose the class maximising the posterior (denominator is common, so ignored).

use: assign the new instance to the most probable class.

ISM CS-4 · p29

Laplace (add-one) smoothing

$$P(X_i=x\mid y)=\frac{\text{count}(x,y)+1}{\text{count}(y)+d}$$

where: \(d\) = number of distinct values of feature \(X_i\) (add 1 to every cell).

use: avoid zero probabilities when a feature value is unseen for a class.

ISM CS-4 · p34–35,44
CS-5 — Random Variables & Distributions · source: S1-26_ISM_CS-5_PPT.pdf

Discrete uniform pmf

$$f(x)=\frac{1}{n},\quad x=1,2,\dots,n$$

where: \(n\) = number of equally likely values.

use: fair die, random digit — all outcomes equally likely.

ISM CS-5 · p8

Valid discrete distribution

$$f(x)\ge 0\quad\text{and}\quad \sum_{x} f(x)=1$$

where: \(f(x)=P(X=x)\).

use: verify a proposed pmf; solve for an unknown probability constant.

ISM CS-5 · p7,9

Expectation (discrete)

$$\mu=E[X]=\sum_{x} x\,f(x)$$

where: sum of value × probability.

use: mean of a RV; a game is fair when \(E[\text{gain}]=0\).

ISM CS-5 · p10–11,13

Variance of a discrete RV

$$\operatorname{Var}(X)=\sum_x (x-\mu)^2 f(x)=E[X^2]-\mu^2,\qquad \sigma=\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X)}$$

where: \(E[X^2]=\sum_x x^2 f(x)\); \(\mu=E[X]\).

use: spread of a RV; the computational form \(E[X^2]-\mu^2\) is fastest.

ISM CS-5 · p12–14

Cumulative distribution function

$$F(x)=P(X\le x)=\sum_{x_i\le x} f(x_i)$$

where: running total of probabilities; \(F(\text{max})=1\).

use: probabilities of "≤" or interval events for a discrete RV.

ISM CS-5 · p15–16

Continuous pdf: validity & probability

$$f(x)\ge 0,\quad \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx=1,\quad P(a\lt X\lt b)=\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$$

where: probability = area under the density; a single point has probability 0.

use: verify a pdf; compute interval probabilities (e.g. \(f(x)=6x^5\) on [0,1]).

ISM CS-5 · p23,27; EC-2 · Q4

Mean & variance (continuous)

$$E[X]=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x\,f(x)\,dx,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(X)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(x-\mu)^2 f(x)\,dx$$

where: integrate over the support of the pdf.

use: expected value and spread of a continuous RV.

ISM CS-5 · p27–28; EC-2 · Q4

Marginal distribution

$$p_X(x)=\sum_{y} p(x,y),\qquad f_X(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x,y)\,dy$$

where: sum/integrate the joint over the other variable.

use: recover one variable's distribution from a joint distribution.

ISM CS-5 · p38,50

Conditional distribution

$$f(y\mid x)=\frac{f(x,y)}{f_X(x)},\qquad f_X(x)>0$$

where: joint divided by the marginal of the conditioning variable.

use: distribution of Y for a fixed X (e.g. \(P(D\mid S=\text{Fever})\)).

ISM CS-5 · p41–44

Independence of two RVs

$$f(x,y)=f_X(x)\,f_Y(y)\ \forall x,y\;\Rightarrow\; E[XY]=E[X]\,E[Y]$$

where: joint factorises into the product of marginals.

use: test independence of RVs; simplify \(E[XY]\).

ISM CS-5 · p40,50
EC Exams — Distributions, Inference, Correlation & Forecasting · source: EC-2 & EC-3 instruction sheets

Binomial distribution

$$P(X=k)=\binom{n}{k}p^{k}(1-p)^{n-k},\quad E[X]=np,\ \operatorname{Var}=np(1-p)$$

where: \(n\) = trials; \(p\) = success probability; \(k\) = number of successes.

use: exactly \(k\) successes in \(n\) independent yes/no trials (e.g. defective bulbs).

EC-2 · Q6

Poisson distribution

$$P(X=k)=\frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^{k}}{k!},\quad \lambda=np,\ E[X]=\operatorname{Var}=\lambda$$

where: \(\lambda\) = mean count; \(k\) = number of events.

use: rare-event counts; approximates binomial when \(n\) large, \(p\) small.

EC-2 · Q6

Continuous uniform distribution

$$f(x)=\frac{1}{b-a},\ a\le x\le b,\quad E[X]=\frac{a+b}{2},\ \operatorname{Var}=\frac{(b-a)^2}{12}$$

where: \(a,b\) = interval endpoints.

use: outcomes equally likely across a continuous interval.

ISM CS-5 · p8 (uniform); course scope

Exponential distribution

$$f(x)=\lambda e^{-\lambda x},\ x\ge 0,\quad E[X]=\frac{1}{\lambda},\ \operatorname{Var}=\frac{1}{\lambda^2}$$

where: \(\lambda\) = rate parameter.

use: waiting times / lifetimes between random events.

Module 3 scope (Prob. Distributions)

Normal distribution (pdf)

$$f(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\,e^{-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}}$$

where: \(\mu\) = mean; \(\sigma\) = standard deviation.

use: continuous bell-shaped data; foundation of the CLT and z-tests.

EC-3 · Q5,Q8

Standard score (z-score)

$$z=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}$$

where: \(x\) = value; \(\mu,\sigma\) = mean and SD.

use: standardise to the \(N(0,1)\) table; count standard deviations from the mean.

EC-3 · Q5

Central limit theorem & standard error

$$\bar{X}\ \dot\sim\ N\!\left(\mu,\ \frac{\sigma^2}{n}\right),\qquad SE=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}$$

where: \(\bar X\) = sample mean; \(n\) = sample size; \(SE\) = standard error of the mean.

use: justify normal-based inference on means for large \(n\); scale of sampling error.

Module 4 scope (Hypothesis Testing)

Confidence interval for a mean

$$\bar{x}\pm z_{\alpha/2}\,\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\quad(\text{or }t_{\alpha/2,\,n-1}\,\tfrac{s}{\sqrt n}\text{ if }\sigma\text{ unknown})$$

where: \(z_{\alpha/2}\) / \(t\) = critical value at confidence \(1-\alpha\).

use: interval estimate of a population mean.

Module 4 scope

Two-sample z-test for means

$$z=\frac{\bar{x}_1-\bar{x}_2}{\sqrt{\dfrac{\sigma_1^2}{n_1}+\dfrac{\sigma_2^2}{n_2}}}$$

where: \(\bar x_i,\sigma_i,n_i\) = mean, population SD and size of sample \(i\).

use: test whether two population means differ (known SDs, large samples) — e.g. two fertilizers.

EC-3 · Q5

One-sample t-statistic

$$t=\frac{\bar{x}-\mu_0}{s/\sqrt{n}},\quad df=n-1$$

where: \(\mu_0\) = hypothesised mean; \(s\) = sample SD.

use: test a mean when the population SD is unknown and \(n\) is small.

Module 4 scope

Chi-square goodness of fit

$$\chi^2=\sum \frac{(O_i-E_i)^2}{E_i}$$

where: \(O_i\) = observed; \(E_i\) = expected frequency.

use: test whether observed counts match an expected distribution / independence in tables.

Module 4 scope

One-way ANOVA (F-test)

$$F=\frac{MS_{\text{between}}}{MS_{\text{within}}}=\frac{SS_{B}/(k-1)}{SS_{W}/(N-k)}$$

where: \(k\) = number of groups; \(N\) = total observations; \(MS\) = mean square.

use: test equality of three or more population means (equal variances).

EC-3 · Q6

Pearson correlation coefficient

$$r=\frac{\sum (x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y)}{\sqrt{\sum (x_i-\bar x)^2\,\sum (y_i-\bar y)^2}}=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)}{s_X s_Y}$$

where: \(\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=\frac{1}{n}\sum(x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y)\); \(r\in[-1,1]\).

use: strength/direction of a linear relationship between two variables.

Module 5 scope (Prediction); EC-3 · Q4

Spearman's rank correlation

$$r_s=1-\frac{6\sum d_i^2}{n(n^2-1)}$$

where: \(d_i\) = difference in ranks of the two variables; \(n\) = number of pairs.

use: monotonic association between ranked data (e.g. two judges' rankings).

EC-3 · Q4

Simple linear regression coefficients

$$\hat{y}=b_0+b_1 x,\qquad b_1=\frac{\sum(x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y)}{\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2},\qquad b_0=\bar y-b_1\bar x$$

where: \(b_1\) = slope; \(b_0\) = intercept (least-squares fit).

use: predict \(y\) from \(x\); the regression/prediction line.

Module 5 scope (Prediction & Forecasting)

Coefficient of determination R²

$$R^2=1-\frac{SS_{\text{res}}}{SS_{\text{tot}}}=1-\frac{\sum(y_i-\hat y_i)^2}{\sum(y_i-\bar y)^2}$$

where: \(SS_{\text{res}}\) = residual sum of squares; \(SS_{\text{tot}}\) = total sum of squares; \(R^2=r^2\) in simple regression.

use: fraction of variance in \(y\) explained by the model.

Module 5 scope

Simple exponential smoothing

$$F_{t+1}=\alpha A_t+(1-\alpha)F_t$$

where: \(F_{t+1}\) = forecast; \(A_t\) = actual at \(t\); \(F_t\) = previous forecast; \(0<\alpha<1\) = smoothing constant.

use: one-step-ahead forecasting of a time series (e.g. daily patient arrivals, α = 0.4).

EC-3 · Q7

Gaussian Mixture Model density

$$p(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\pi_k\,\frac{1}{\sigma_k\sqrt{2\pi}}\,e^{-\frac{(x-\mu_k)^2}{2\sigma_k^2}},\quad \sum_k \pi_k=1$$

where: \(\pi_k\) = mixing coefficient; \(\mu_k,\sigma_k^2\) = component mean and variance.

use: model multi-modal densities; evaluate mixture density at a point (fit via EM).

Properties of expectation

$$E(aX)=aE(X),\qquad E(X+b)=E(X)+b,\qquad E(aX+b)=aE(X)+b$$ $$E(XY)=E(X)\,E(Y)\ \text{only if }X,Y\text{ are independent}$$

where: \(a,b\) = constants; \(X,Y\) = random variables.

use: rescale/shift a random variable's mean; caution: \(E(X/Y)\neq E(X)/E(Y)\) in general.

ISM CS-5 · p10 (image)

Properties of variance

$$\operatorname{Var}(b)=0,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(aX)=a^2\operatorname{Var}(X),\qquad \sigma_{aX}=|a|\,\sigma_X$$ $$\operatorname{Var}(aX+bY)=a^2\operatorname{Var}(X)+b^2\operatorname{Var}(Y)\quad(X,Y\text{ independent})$$

where: \(a,b\) = constants; \(\sigma\) = standard deviation.

use: variance under linear transforms; adding a constant leaves spread unchanged; combine independent RVs.

ISM CS-5 · p12 (image)

Naïve Bayes for text classification (with Laplace smoothing)

$$P(c\mid d)=P(c)\prod_{t_i\in d} P(t_i\mid c),\qquad P(t_i\mid c)=\frac{N_{ic}+1}{N_c+T}$$

where: \(d\) = document of terms \(t_i\); \(N_{ic}\) = number of times term \(t_i\) appears in documents of class \(c\); \(N_c\) = total number of terms in all documents of class \(c\); \(T\) = vocabulary size (number of unique words); \(P(c)\) = fraction of documents in class \(c\).

use: classify a document (spam vs normal, positive vs negative) from word frequencies (bag of words); the \(+1\) numerator and \(+T\) denominator keep every term probability non-zero and bounded below 1.
